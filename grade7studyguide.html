Core Digital Words (Tech Basics)

Hardware

The physical parts of a computer or phone that you can actually touch with your hands. Example: Keyboard, mouse, monitor screen, or a printer.

Software

The apps, games, and programs inside a device that tell the hardware what to do. You cannot touch software. Example: Microsoft Word, Google Chrome, CapCut, or Minecraft.

Cloud Computing

Saving and opening your files or apps over the internet instead of keeping them on just one computer's hard drive. Example: Saving homework on Google Drive at school and opening it at home.

Network

Two or more computers or devices connected together (with cables or Wi-Fi) so they can share files, internet, or printers. Example: All the computers in our school lab sharing one printer.

Bandwidth

Think of bandwidth like the width of a highway. A wider highway lets more cars travel fast—higher bandwidth lets more data move quickly over the internet without lagging! Example: High bandwidth lets a whole class watch HD videos without buffering.

Hardware Specs & Cybersecurity

Hardware Specs: How powerful a computer's inside parts are (like its processor "brain" and RAM memory).
Cybersecurity: The digital locks, passwords, and safety rules that protect computers and private info from hackers.

2

Digital Tools at Work

Different jobs need special software to get work done faster and with fewer mistakes. Remember these 4 main industries:

Healthcare

EHR Systems

Electronic Health Records let doctors and nurses look up a patient's allergies and medical history in seconds instead of hunting for paper folders.

Education

LMS Platforms

Learning Management Systems (like Google Classroom) let teachers post assignments and students submit homework and check grades from anywhere.

Engineering

CAD Software

Computer-Aided Design lets architects and engineers draw detailed 2D and 3D building blueprints on a screen so they can easily fix changes without wasting paper.

Finance

Banking & Spreadsheets

Encrypted online banking portals and spreadsheet apps help banks and accountants track money safely and calculate totals automatically.

What Does "Cost-Effective" Mean?

A digital tool is cost-effective when it is worth the money because it saves hours of hard work, reduces paper waste, and prevents costly mistakes—even if the software isn't free!

3

Being Safe & Smart Online (Digital Citizenship)

👣 Digital Footprint

The permanent trail of posts, photos, comments, and websites you leave behind whenever you go online. Even if you hit "delete," someone may have already saved or screenshotted it!

🎣 Phishing

Fake emails, texts, or links pretending to be a real company (like a bank or game site) to trick you into typing your password or personal info. Red flags: Urgent threats, weird web links, and spelling mistakes.

📝 Plagiarism

Copying someone else's words, schoolwork, or pictures from the internet and pretending you wrote them yourself without giving credit.

🚫 Software Piracy & Cyberbullying

Software Piracy: Illegally copying or downloading paid apps, movies, or games for free.
Cyberbullying: Using phones, games, or social media to repeatedly tease, threaten, or hurt someone's feelings.

Making Videos & Writing Reports

1. Recording Good Video

  • Landscape Mode: Always hold the phone sideways for reports.
  • Good Lighting: Put the light in front of the speaker's face, never behind them.
  • Clear Audio: Record in a quiet place close to the microphone.
  • Rule of Thirds: Line up the person neatly with a little space above their head (headroom).

2. Video Editing Words

  • Trimming: Cutting off awkward pauses or mistakes at the start or end of a clip.
  • Cropping: Zooming in or adjusting frame edges to cut out distracting background clutter.
  • Timeline: The track where you line up your video and sound clips in order.
  • Exporting: Saving your finished project into a playable video file (like an MP4).

3. Interview Reports

  • Qualitative Data: Information made of people's spoken opinions, ideas, and answers from interviews.
  • Supporting Evidence: True facts or quotes from your interviews that prove why digital skills help people.
  • Misconceptions: False myths or wrong beliefs people have about technology that your report corrects.
